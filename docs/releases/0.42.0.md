# Arugula 0.42.0

Driving an agent on someone else's machine now takes their trust, and
control records how it's used.

## Editors need trust to drive agents (#976)

On a machine that isn't yours, being an Editor on the session no longer
lets you drive its agents. Prompting an agent, `cd`, approving or denying
a tool, answering a card, rerunning a command and accepting an edit now
need what typing already needed: the owner's trust on that pane.

- Refusals say so in the same words as typing, and **Ask … for 30
  minutes** in the follow-up box and the pane menu asks for that trust.
- Your own VM, and the agent you started on it, still need none.
  Another guest's VM, or the owner's, now does.
- In a conversation, `@lead` and answers to the lead's cards reach the
  lead only from someone the owner trusts with the lead's block.
  Anyone else's `@lead` is posted as a comment.
- Not changed: owners, and team Editors on a team's machines. Gates,
  decisions, forge reviews and a page's questions stay Editor-only.

## Control: product analytics (#977)

Control now records what's done with it: signing in, adding devices and
machines, teams and invites, relayed connections (how long, how many
bytes), billing, hosted sandboxes and cloud runs, and requests that
change something or fail. Records go to PostHog under your account's
random id, sent from control's server: no cookie, nothing in your
browser. They never hold your name, GitHub username, email or IP
address, or anything from your terminals, prompts, repositories,
secrets, invite links or join codes. The
[privacy notice](https://arugula.io/privacy) says what's sent and how to
have it deleted.

Self-hosted controls send nothing unless they're given a PostHog key
(`POSTHOG_API_KEY`, [control-ops.md](../control-ops.md#product-analytics-posthog)).
